About Dr. Megan Kunze

A career built around children, families, and meaningful intervention.

For more than three decades, Dr. Megan Kunze has dedicated her career to improving the lives of young children with developmental differences and the families who support them. Her work has taken her from classrooms and community programs to interdisciplinary clinical teams, university research settings, and international collaborations, all with a shared goal: helping children thrive through compassionate, evidence-based care.

Her approach blends research, clinical practice, and coaching so families and educators can use strategies that fit real life.

Professional biography

Building a career that bridges practice and research

Dr. Kunze is an Assistant Research Professor in the Department of Psychology at the University of Oregon and serves as Associate Director of the Center on Brain Injury Research and Training (CBIRT). She also holds affiliations with the Department of Special Education and Clinical Sciences and the Prevention Science Institute.

Her research centers on early intervention, autism, traumatic brain injury, applied behavior analysis, and family-focused naturalistic interventions. Rather than asking families to fit into a rigid model, she develops approaches that fit naturally into everyday routines while respecting each family's values, language, goals, and strengths.

Much of her recent work focuses on caregiver coaching, telehealth delivery, and accessible intervention models for rural families, military families, and culturally and linguistically diverse communities. She is especially interested in helping families receive support earlier and in ways that feel practical, respectful, and sustainable.

Career path

From early childhood education to university leadership

Dr. Kunze earned her bachelor's degree in Family and Human Development from Ohio State University and her master's degree in Special Education with a focus on Early Intervention and Early Childhood from the University of Texas at Austin. Before entering university faculty roles, she worked in classrooms, child development centers, and community programs, including curriculum leadership for military child development centers in Germany.

She later founded and operated MK Behavioral Center in Texas, where she partnered with families, schools, and clinicians to create individualized intervention plans rooted in applied behavior analysis. She also served on interdisciplinary autism diagnostic teams and provided direct coaching to caregivers and educators, experiences that continue to shape the way she thinks about meaningful, usable research.

Today, her work continues to reflect that same philosophy: strong research should improve everyday life for children and the adults who care for them.

Current focus

Supporting young children with autism, traumatic brain injury, and developmental differences through family-centered research and intervention.

Method

Naturalistic applied behavior analysis, telehealth coaching, and interventions shaped around family values and daily routines.

Impact

Connecting university research, professional preparation, and community partnerships so evidence-based support reaches more families.

Why her work matters

Helping families feel supported, not overwhelmed

Dr. Kunze's work is especially focused on families who have historically faced barriers to services, including rural communities and families who need culturally and linguistically responsive support. Through her research and clinical collaborations, she helps build interventions that are more accessible, more humane, and more likely to fit the realities of daily family life.

She also teaches graduate courses, mentors students, supervises future behavior analysts, and collaborates internationally through Autism ECHO Oregon, Ukraine ECHO, and a Fulbright Specialist assignment supporting autism services in Azerbaijan. Across all of these roles, she remains committed to the idea that the most valuable research is research that can be used well by families, educators, and clinicians.
