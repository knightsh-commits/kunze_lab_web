✨ About Dr. Kunze

Advancing early intervention, applied behavior analysis, and family-focused naturalistic interventions to support young children with developmental differences.

Dr. Megan Kunze is an Assistant Research Professor at the University of Oregon, serving as the Associate Director of the Center on Brain Injury Research and Training (CBIRT) within the Department of Psychology. She holds core affiliations across the College of Education's Department of Special Education and Clinical Sciences and the Prevention Science Institute.

With over 30 years of experience supporting children, families, and early childhood specialists, Dr. Kunze is a Board Certified Behavior Analyst-Doctorate (BCBA-D) and Licensed Behavior Analyst. Her clinical leadership spans directing inclusive child development centers, early intervention facilities, and therapeutic clinics. She serves as a vital community resource and behavior expert for both the Autism ECHO for Oregon and the Ukraine ECHO panels, extending clinical knowledge to medical and educational professionals worldwide.

🔬 Research & Lab Projects

Click on any project box below to expand full details and methodologies.

This project adapts the Tiered Online Training and Support (TOTS) parent training framework to address the specific challenges faced by military, reserve, and veteran families during deployment and family reunification. The research focuses on deploying an evidence-based, fully online coaching interface aimed at decreasing child behavioral difficulties and bolstering psychological resiliency at the family unit level.

Promoting Reciprocal Relationships with Flexibility, Coaching, and Teaching (PRRFCT Match) evaluates parent-mediated early intervention delivery via telehealth. Utilizing early career intervention specialists, this program targets the expansion of flexible play environments and interactive behaviors for young children with intellectual and developmental disabilities. The project includes a dedicated linguistic and cultural adaptation program specifically tailored for Spanish-speaking families.

A multi-tiered state and community-driven initiative focused on traumatic brain injury (TBI) awareness, identification, and care tracking. This research develops caregiver and educational curricula stretching from early childhood settings through high schools, ensuring consistent infrastructure across school, medical, family, and legislative components.

This validation research centers on utilizing screening protocols to identify head injuries and subsequent executive functioning constraints among justice-involved youth. The core goal of this research is validating systemic alternatives highlighting early neuro-educational intervention over structural incarceration pathways.
